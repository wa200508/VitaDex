# Ohio starter collection

Prepared 2026-11-02 for early readers and adult co-explorers. Five entries replace
fictional creatures in the default catalog. All facts were compared with the
references by Codex; independent human review, child reading tests, and field
identification testing are still pending. None of these cards confirms what the
camera saw: capture/identification is not implemented.

Introductions are short original summaries, about 30–40 words each. Sources,
field-level citation mappings, access dates, credit, brief evidence excerpts and
excerpt checksums are bundled with the catalog and saved with each card.

## What children see and hear

Each simulated first discovery saves a card and requests the name and short
introduction aloud. Repeated species stay quiet. The card offers replay, a
stylized illustration, simple facts, and a Sources & Information section. No
fantasy moves are assigned to these real organisms. “Starter” is a collection
label, not a conservation-status claim.

The source status is explicit: **SOURCE CHECKED / human review pending**. Source
checking is not peer review, and reference metadata validation is not a fact check.

## Source boundaries and reuse

These references support the fields mapped to them in `data/database.json`, not
all possible questions about an organism. A brief excerpt and a section locator
make the evidence easier to inspect. Excerpt checksums preserve local integrity;
most reference pages do not provide a frozen revision URL and may change later.
The cards preserve the source record they were created with.

Only short attributed excerpts are bundled, alongside original factual summaries.
OPLIN's site identifies CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 US terms. Other source records do not
assert a bulk-text redistribution license. Full source ingestion for chat will
need a separate content and reuse review. No source photographs or recordings
are copied; the vector illustrations are original and intentionally stylized.

The unreachable ODNR species page and blocked Ohio State fact sheets were not
used as references for card claims. OPLIN and the accessible extension resources
provide the Ohio-specific context instead.

## Before mobile release

- Have an independent human editor check each source-to-claim mapping and any
  source changes, including scientific-name alternatives.
- Read the introductions with a six-year-old and revise unclear vocabulary.
- Confirm speech pronunciation, timing, and controls on a real phone.
- Keep general observe-from-a-distance guidance separate from organism facts.
  The cards provide no advice about touching, feeding, edibility, or medical use.
- Keep manual simulation explicit until a separately tested identification flow exists.
- Add more familiar organisms only with the same evidence and attribution checks.
